Opening

The Sign of the Cross

In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.

Novena Opening Offering (in Petition)

Hail, Queen of the Most Holy Rosary, my Mother Mary, hail! At thy feet I humbly kneel to offer thee a Crown of Roses, bright-yellow roses to remind thee of the light of thy Son's public life, each bud recalling to thee a holy mystery; each ten bound together with my petition for a particular grace. O Holy Queen, dispenser of God's graces, and Mother of all who invoke thee! Thou canst not look upon my gift and fail to see its binding. As thou receivest my gift, so wilt thou receive my petition; from thy bounty thou wilt give me the favor I so earnestly and trustingly seek: the favor I place in your hands. I despair of nothing that I ask of thee. Show thyself my Mother!

PRAYER OF INTENTIONS

Lord Jesus, through the hands of Your Blessed Mother, I offer this Rosary for these intentions:

  • For this channel, that it may grow, bear good fruit, and become a way for me to serve You full-time.
  • For all our vocations, to the priesthood, religious life, or marriage, and for the safety and holiness of our future spouses and families.
  • For our stream family, all our lost sheep, and every viewer's private intentions, that they may grow in holiness and be protected.
  • For the sick, and for good health for our friends, families, and viewers.
  • For myself: for healing, for patience, fortitude, humility, and purity, and for a good, steady job.
  • For all who are suffering, struggling with addiction, despairing, or in need of hope.
  • For all who are far from God, and for those of other faiths, that they may come to know Jesus personally.
  • For our enemies and all who speak against us, that they may come to love Jesus, and that we may love them without hatred.

And in thanksgiving for every blessing You have already given, and for those who guide and care for us.

Surrender Novena (Don Dolindo Ruotolo) · Day 2 of 9

Day 2

Surrender to me does not mean to fret, to be upset, or to lose hope, nor does it mean offering to me a worried prayer asking me to follow you and change your worry into prayer. It is against this surrender, deeply against it, to worry, to be nervous and to desire to think about the consequences of anything. It is like the confusion that children feel when they ask their mother to see to their needs, and then try to take care of those needs for themselves so that their childlike efforts get in their mother’s way. Surrender means to placidly close the eyes of the soul, to turn away from thoughts of tribulation and to put yourself in my care, so that only I act, saying, “You take care of it.”

Ten times

O Jesus, I surrender myself to you, take care of everything!

To Our Lady

Mother, I am yours now and forever. Through you and with you I always want to belong completely to Jesus.

1And the Lord said to Moses: “I will touch Pharaoh and Egypt with one more plague, and after these things he will release you, and he will compel you to go out. 2Therefore, you will tell all the people to ask, a man of his friend, and a woman of her neighbor, for vessels of silver and of gold. 3Then the Lord will grant favor to his people in the sight of the Egyptians.” And Moses was a very great man in the land of Egypt, in the sight of the servants of Pharaoh and of all the people. 4And he said: “Thus says the Lord: ‘In the middle of the night I will enter into Egypt. 5And every firstborn in the land of the Egyptians shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh, who sits on his throne, even to the firstborn of the handmaid, who is at the millstone, and all the firstborn of the beasts of burden. 6And there will be a great outcry throughout the entire land of Egypt, such as has not been before, nor ever will be afterward. 7But among all the sons of Israel there shall not be even a mutter from a dog, from man, even to cattle, so that you may know how miraculously the Lord divides the Egyptians from Israel.’ 8And all these, your servants, shall descend to me and shall reverence me, by saying: ‘Depart, you and all the people who are subject to you.’ After these things, we will depart.” 9And he went out from Pharaoh exceedingly angry. Day 33 · Locusts, Darkness, and Death of the Firstborn
Readings: Exodus 10–11, Leviticus 8, Psalm 50
Timeline period: Egypt and Exodus

Reflection

Also on Day 33: the episode summary says Fr. Mike shows how the ten plagues reveal that God is far greater than the gods of Egypt. Exodus 11 is the short, quiet chapter before the storm. God tells Moses there is one plague left, and after it Pharaoh will not just let Israel go but drive them out. The people are to ask their Egyptian neighbors for silver and gold, and God gives them favor in Egypt's eyes. Moses announces that at midnight every firstborn in Egypt will die, from Pharaoh's heir on the throne to the son of the slave girl at the millstone, while among Israel not even a dog will growl. Then he walks out from Pharaoh burning with anger, and Pharaoh's heart stays hard.

Notes
  • Catechism 304. Scripture often credits actions straight to God without naming the secondary causes. This is not a primitive way of speaking but a deep way of recalling that God is first and Lord of history, which is how to read “the Lord hardened the heart of Pharaoh” in verse 10.
  • St. Augustine, cited by Haydock (after Calmet) on Exodus 11:4: On God's word “I will enter into Egypt,” Haydock notes that the Fathers read it as God acting through His angels: St. Chrysostom points to a good angel (Wisdom 18), while St. Augustine points to Psalm 77 (78):49, where God sends His wrath “by evil angels”.
  • Haydock on Exodus 11:2. The Israelites are to ask for vessels of silver and gold, not “borrow” them as some translations put it, which is what God had already commanded in chapter 3.
  • Haydock on Exodus 11:5. The slave grinding at the mill was the lowest of servants, yet the firstborn of Pharaoh and of the slave are named together: God makes no distinction between king and beggar, and death levels all.
For prayer

Death levels the king and the slave alike: what am I holding on to as if it will last, and what would I let go of if I remembered that?
