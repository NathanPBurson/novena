PRAYER OF INTENTIONS

Lord Jesus, through the hands of Your Blessed Mother, I offer this Rosary for these intentions:

  • For this channel, that it may grow, bear good fruit, and become a way for me to serve You full-time.
  • For all our vocations, to the priesthood, religious life, or marriage, and for the safety and holiness of our future spouses and families.
  • For our stream family, all our lost sheep, and every viewer's private intentions, that they may grow in holiness and be protected.
  • For the sick, and for good health for our friends, families, and viewers.
  • For myself: for healing, for patience, fortitude, humility, and purity, and for a good, steady job.
  • For all who are suffering, struggling with addiction, despairing, or in need of hope.
  • For all who are far from God, and for those of other faiths, that they may come to know Jesus personally.
  • For our enemies and all who speak against us, that they may come to love Jesus, and that we may love them without hatred.

And in thanksgiving for every blessing You have already given, and for those who guide and care for us.

Closing

Spiritual Communion

My Jesus, I believe that Thou art present in the Most Holy Sacrament. I love Thee above all things, and I desire to receive Thee into my soul. Since I cannot at this moment receive Thee sacramentally, come at least spiritually into my heart. I embrace Thee as if Thou wert already there and unite myself wholly to Thee. Never permit me to be separated from Thee. Amen.

Hail, Holy Queen

Hail, Holy Queen, Mother of Mercy, our life, our sweetness, and our hope. To Thee do we cry, poor banished children of Eve. To Thee do we send up our sighs, mourning and weeping in this valley of tears. Turn then, most gracious advocate, Thine eyes of mercy towards us. And after this our exile, show unto us the blessed Fruit of Thy womb, Jesus. Oh clement, Oh loving, Oh sweet Virgin Mary.

Let us pray

O God! Whose only-begotten Son, by His life, death, and resurrection, has purchased for us the rewards of eternal life; grant, we beseech Thee, that, meditating on these mysteries of the Most Holy Rosary of the Blessed Virgin Mary, we may both imitate what they contain and obtain what they promise, through the same Christ our Lord. Amen.

May God rebuke him, we humbly pray; and do thou, O Prince of the heavenly host, by the power of God, cast into hell Satan and all the evil spirits who prowl about the world seeking the ruin of souls. Amen.

St. Padre Pio

St. Padre Pio, faithful son of St. Francis, teach me to trust Jesus completely and to offer my sufferings in union with His. Watch over this channel and our stream family, and help me to pray, hope, and not worry. Amen.

St. Joan of Arc

St. Joan of Arc, brave and faithful servant of God, obtain for me courage, honesty, and faithfulness. Help me stand firm against every enemy without hatred, and to finish this novena strong. Amen.

St. Camillus de Lellis

No memorial in the US calendar today (Thursday of the 31st Week in Ordinary Time). Camillus, a soldier whose gambling addiction cost him everything, converted and founded an order to nurse the sick, so he is a fitting patron for our prayers for the sick and for all who struggle with addiction.

St. Camillus de Lellis, once lost to gambling and then given wholly to the service of the sick, pray for us, that we may be freed from every chain, care for the suffering as Christ cares for us, and persevere to the end. Amen.

Prayer for Purity (to St. Joseph)

Guardian of virgins, and holy father Joseph, to whose faithful custody Christ Jesus, Innocence itself, and Mary, Virgin of virgins, were committed; I pray and beseech thee, by these dear pledges, Jesus and Mary, that, being preserved from all uncleanness, I may with spotless mind, pure heart, and chaste body, ever serve Jesus and Mary most chastely all the days of my life. Amen.

Scripture · Exodus 11 (CPDV)

1And the Lord said to Moses: “I will touch Pharaoh and Egypt with one more plague, and after these things he will release you, and he will compel you to go out. 2Therefore, you will tell all the people to ask, a man of his friend, and a woman of her neighbor, for vessels of silver and of gold. 3Then the Lord will grant favor to his people in the sight of the Egyptians.” And Moses was a very great man in the land of Egypt, in the sight of the servants of Pharaoh and of all the people. 4And he said: “Thus says the Lord: ‘In the middle of the night I will enter into Egypt. 5And every firstborn in the land of the Egyptians shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh, who sits on his throne, even to the firstborn of the handmaid, who is at the millstone, and all the firstborn of the beasts of burden. 6And there will be a great outcry throughout the entire land of Egypt, such as has not been before, nor ever will be afterward. 7But among all the sons of Israel there shall not be even a mutter from a dog, from man, even to cattle, so that you may know how miraculously the Lord divides the Egyptians from Israel.’ 8And all these, your servants, shall descend to me and shall reverence me, by saying: ‘Depart, you and all the people who are subject to you.’ After these things, we will depart.” 9And he went out from Pharaoh exceedingly angry. Then the Lord said to Moses: “Pharaoh will not listen to you, so that many signs may be accomplished in the land of Egypt.” 10Now Moses and Aaron did all the wonders that are written, in the sight of Pharaoh. And the Lord hardened the heart of Pharaoh; neither did he release the sons of Israel from his land.

Bible in a Year · Day 33
Where we are

Day 33 · Locusts, Darkness, and Death of the Firstborn
Readings: Exodus 10–11, Leviticus 8, Psalm 50
Timeline period: Egypt and Exodus

Reflection

Also on Day 33: the episode summary says Fr. Mike shows how the ten plagues reveal that God is far greater than the gods of Egypt. Exodus 11 is the short, quiet chapter before the storm. God tells Moses there is one plague left, and after it Pharaoh will not just let Israel go but drive them out. The people are to ask their Egyptian neighbors for silver and gold, and God gives them favor in Egypt's eyes. Moses announces that at midnight every firstborn in Egypt will die, from Pharaoh's heir on the throne to the son of the slave girl at the millstone, while among Israel not even a dog will growl. Then he walks out from Pharaoh burning with anger, and Pharaoh's heart stays hard.

Notes
  • Catechism 304. Scripture often credits actions straight to God without naming the secondary causes. This is not a primitive way of speaking but a deep way of recalling that God is first and Lord of history, which is how to read “the Lord hardened the heart of Pharaoh” in verse 10.
  • St. Augustine, cited by Haydock (after Calmet) on Exodus 11:4: On God's word “I will enter into Egypt,” Haydock notes that the Fathers read it as God acting through His angels: St. Chrysostom points to a good angel (Wisdom 18), while St. Augustine points to Psalm 77 (78):49, where God sends His wrath “by evil angels”.
  • Haydock on Exodus 11:2. The Israelites are to ask for vessels of silver and gold, not “borrow” them as some translations put it, which is what God had already commanded in chapter 3.
  • Haydock on Exodus 11:5. The slave grinding at the mill was the lowest of servants, yet the firstborn of Pharaoh and of the slave are named together: God makes no distinction between king and beggar, and death levels all.
For prayer

Death levels the king and the slave alike: what am I holding on to as if it will last, and what would I let go of if I remembered that?
